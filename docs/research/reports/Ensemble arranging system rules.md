# Give every instrument one job, then thin

Professional arrangers keep a band from sounding like a jumble by working in a small number of **functional layers**, not in instruments. The layers are primary melody, an optional secondary line or countermelody, harmony (sustained pad or rhythmic comp), bass, and percussion. Each layer gets its own instrument or group, its own register, its own rhythm and, where the ensemble allows, its own colour. Any instrument beyond those layers **doubles** a layer at the unison or octave rather than starting a new one. Producers put the ceiling at about **four distinct layers at once, five at most with drums counted as one** ([Owsinski](https://bobbyowsinskiblog.com/arrangement-rules/); [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/arranging-pop)). Clarity then comes from four mechanisms that can be coded. Voices are spaced like the harmonic series (wide intervals low, close intervals high), and nothing closer than a fifth goes below its low interval limit. Accompaniment stays below the melody and attacks where the melody holds or rests. Melody gets the most weight, using Rimsky-Korsakov's balance ratios. Texture changes across the form: thin verses, layers added on each repeat, a strip-back before the last climax, and an ending planned in advance. Turkish practice turns the Western model upside down. Folk, fasıl and mehter ensembles play **one melody heterophonically in unison and octaves** over a continuous usul, and harmony is optional, either Western or İlerici's quartal system ([Kınık 2011](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/219568)). So the engine needs a second texture mode, not just new presets. The research systems and arranger keyboards agree on the architecture. Fix the harmonic plan, give each part a role and a rhythm pattern for each phrase, then realise the pitches deterministically under range, monophony and ordering constraints. Smooth transitions should count for more than local fit, and repeated sections should reuse their textures ([Pachet & Roy 2001](https://www.francoispachet.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/pachet-01-Musical_Harmonization_with_Constraints.pdf); [AccoMontage](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.11213)). The specification at the end turns all of this into roles, patterns, a section plan, balance values, constraints, automated checks and controls. Each item is marked by how well it is sourced.

## Four layers, one job each: the role vocabulary every tradition converges on

Orchestration texts, analysis courses, jazz arranging, arranger keyboards and machine-learning datasets all arrive at nearly the same role vocabulary. Rubin's "Type I" orchestration gives each layer "in effect, portrayed by an instrument or instrumental group": primary melody, secondary melodic voice, accompaniment and bass. A new section may set up a different hierarchy ([Rubin, UMN Duluth](https://www.d.umn.edu/~jrubin1/JHR%20Orchestration%20I1.htm)). Analysis courses refine this into primary melody, secondary melody, parallel supporting melody (the tune in thirds or sixths), static support (pedal), harmonic support, rhythmic support and harmonic-rhythmic support ([Music Theory Notes](https://www.academia.edu/882388/Music_Theory_Notes_Ch_7_17)). Jazz arrangers name the backing figures **pads, stabs/riffs and countermelody** ([Taming the Saxophone](https://tamingthesaxophone.com/theory/arranging/jazz-backings)). Yamaha's style engine fixes the channels as Rhythm 1/2, Bass, Chord 1/2 ("rhythmic chord backing, typically piano or guitar"), Pad ("sustained sounds such as strings, organ, and choir") and Phrase 1/2 ("punchy brass stabs, arpeggiated chords, and other extras") ([Style Creation Tutorial](https://www.scribd.com/document/95069076/Style-Creation-Tutorial); [Genos manual snippet](https://manualshive.com/en/manual/yamaha/genos/897212?page=130)). Annotated symphony datasets label each instrument per bar as **melody, rhythm, harmony or mixed** ([S3, ISMIR 2024](https://ismir2024program.ismir.net/lbd_463.html)). Couturier's piano-texture syntax adds what roles leave out: a density (voice count), an intra-layer relation (homorhythm, parallel, octave) and a figure (sustained, repeated, oscillating, scalar) ([Couturier et al., ISMIR 2022](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2022/paper/000061.pdf)).

Two consequences follow for Airfret. The current `BandRole` (melody, double, counter, harmony, bass, rhythm) already matches this consensus, and the `CompPattern` set (afterbeat, arpeggio, held) is the right shape. What the consensus adds is a split between the **role** (what the part means) and the **figure and rhythm skeleton** (how it moves). AccoMontage-3 defines a track's "orchestral function" purely as its onset-count rhythm pattern ([Zhao et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.16334)), and Yamaha keeps the pattern separate from the transposition rule. The second consequence is that **the unit of clutter is a distinct rhythmic idea, not an instrument**. Owsinski counts a doubled guitar or a lead plus two harmonies as one element, but two guitars playing different parts as two ([Owsinski](https://bobbyowsinskiblog.com/arrangement-rules/)). The engine should therefore cap *distinct active roles*, at 4 by default and configurable from 3 to 5. When the user adds a sixth instrument, it doubles an existing role and does not get a new line. This is also the cheapest way to make a section sound bigger without clutter. Thin textures (melody alone, melody plus bass) are underused in band writing ([ArrangeMe](https://blog.arrangeme.com/blog/arranging-for-marching-and-pep-bands)), so the cap is a maximum, not a target.

Colour contrast between layers is the other rule with a strong source. Rimsky-Korsakov wants the harmonic basis to differ from the melody "in colour as well as in fullness and intensity" ([Rimsky-Korsakov](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm)). Backing for a featured trombone should come from the saxes, not the remaining trombones ([scoredchanges.com](https://www.scoredchanges.com/how-to/backings.pdf)). Airfret's catalogue makes this hard: guitar, saz, kanun and piano are all plucked or struck and decay fast. When the family rule cannot be met, the engine has to separate roles by **register and articulation** instead, for example a held bowed or wind pad under a plucked lead, or a plucked comp under a sustained wind melody. That substitution is an inference, not a sourced rule.

## Clarity is engineered: doubling, spacing, rhythm and weight

### Doubling: the tune is doubled upward, the bass downward, never the inner parts

Rimsky-Korsakov's doubling rules are the most concrete in the notes. Harmony is normally four real parts. Extra instruments double the **soprano and alto in octaves**. Doubling the tenor makes the writing close, and doubling the bass sounds heavy, so the bass is doubled only in the lower octave. Consecutive octaves between upper parts are forbidden unless they come from a declared doubling ([Rimsky-Korsakov](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm)). A unison double adds "power and richness" without changing the colour. An octave double adds brilliance, but splitting one section into octaves halves the resonance of each line ([Rimsky-Korsakov](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm)). An inner melodic voice "could be aurally lost", so its distinct timbre matters more than the number of players on it ([Rubin](https://www.d.umn.edu/~jrubin1/JHR%20Orchestration%20I1.htm)). When one instrument is featured, the rest of its section should drop out, and no single backing instrument should break into an individual line even for a few notes ([Evan Rogers](https://evanrogersmusic.com/blog-contents/big-band-arranging/open-voicings?format=amp); [scoredchanges.com](https://www.scoredchanges.com/how-to/backings.pdf)). The wind-band orchestration study shows how a machine fails here. A flute that took "only some notes of the melody" was judged inappropriate ([Nabeoka et al. 2023](https://zenodo.org/records/10113109/files/cmmr2023_3c-P5.pdf)). The lead must own **every** melody note in a phrase, folded by octaves if necessary, or none of them.

### Spacing and low interval limits

Rimsky-Korsakov takes the harmonic series as the model for chord layout. "The bass should rarely lie at a greater distance than an octave from the part directly above it," and "nothing is worse than writing chords, the upper and lower parts of which are separated by wide, empty intervals" ([Rimsky-Korsakov](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm)). The octave rule is stricter than chorale practice, so it should be a soft cost, not a hard limit. Low interval limits (LIL) are "the lowest possible position they can be played together without sounding muddy". They are guidelines: consonances can sit lower than dissonances, the bass root counts as present even when another instrument plays it, and loud low brass is less forgiving than soft low strings ([Robin Hoffmann](https://www.robin-hoffmann.com/tutorials/low-interval-limits/)). Sweetwater gives the major third's limit as B1–D♯2 in its own convention, where middle C is C3 ([Sweetwater](https://www.sweetwater.com/insync/low-interval-limit/)). In scientific pitch (C4 = middle C) the verified lower bounds are **m2 ≥ E3, M2 ≥ E♭3, m3 ≥ C3, M3 ≥ B2**. The rest of the commonly reproduced chart (P4 ≈ A2, tritone ≈ B2, P5 ≈ B♭1, sixths and sevenths ≈ F2) could not be read from a source and must be treated as unverified. Big-band practice agrees in substance: no low intervals "other than octaves and 5ths" in pads ([Evan Rogers](https://evanrogersmusic.com/blog-contents/big-band-arranging/open-voicings?format=amp)). Berklee keeps tensions above G3, allows the third down to E3, and says close voicings let the melody stand out while wide ones broaden the sound ([Berklee](https://www.berklee.edu/node/2436901)).

### Keep the backing under the tune and out of its way

"Don't let the pad cover the melody" ([Evan Rogers](https://evanrogersmusic.com/blog-contents/big-band-arranging/open-voicings?format=amp)). Backing parts should avoid the lead's register, and fills belong *between* phrases ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/arranging-pop)). The sources allow two exceptions. A melody on a much stronger instrument can sit inside the backing ([Taming the Saxophone](https://tamingthesaxophone.com/theory/arranging/jazz-backings)), and a countermelody may cross the melody if it uses a different timbre. No source gives a numeric separation margin. The notes propose keeping the top accompaniment voice at least a minor third below the current melody note, and a full octave below when the two share a family. That is a reasonable default, but it is a proposal.

### Rhythmic complementarity is the strongest anti-jumble rule

The most widely repeated clarity rule concerns rhythm, not pitch. "The counterpoint should sustain while the melody is moving and vice versa" ([Taming the Saxophone](https://tamingthesaxophone.com/theory/arranging/jazz-backings)). Accompaniment should "move when the melody does not" ([Athletic Bands handout](https://www.scribd.com/document/468918359/Athletic-Bands-pdf)). Stabs "nearly always fill the gaps in and around the melody", and placed over the tune "they may confuse the melody" ([Taming the Saxophone](https://tamingthesaxophone.com/theory/arranging/jazz-backings)). The bass owns the strong beats, and chord instruments take the afterbeats: the oom-pah, stride and folk bass-strum patterns all share this logic ([LibreTexts 14.5](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Theory/Music_Theory_for_the_21st-Century_Classroom_(Hutchinson)/14%3A_Accompanimental_Textures/14.05%3A_Afterbeats_and_Offbeats); [Celtic Guitar Music](https://celticguitarmusic.com/tb_backup_styles.htm)). AccoMontage-3's learned arrangements show the same split without being told to: **strings on the downbeat, brass on the offbeat**, and **two guitars with complementary roles, one melodic and one harmonic** ([Zhao et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.16334)). An engine can enforce this with an onset grid. It computes the melody's activity map, places countermelody motion and punctuation only in melody holds of a beat or longer and in rests, and caps the number of non-drum roles attacking on one subdivision. The cap value (two, except on accented tutti hits) is an inference.

### Voice leading of inner parts

Pads and comps should keep common tones, move each voice to the nearest chord tone, keep the same number of voices on every chord, include the guide tones (3rd, and 7th if the chord has one), and avoid minor seconds and minor ninths in stable chords ([Evan Rogers](https://evanrogersmusic.com/blog-contents/big-band-arranging/open-voicings?format=amp); [Berklee](https://www.berklee.edu/node/2436901); [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/smoother-chord-progressions-voice-leading)). "Large leaps make a part stand out against the melody" ([Evan Rogers](https://evanrogersmusic.com/blog-contents/big-band-arranging/open-voicings?format=amp)). The sources disagree on the pad's lowest note. Rogers puts the root at the bottom by default, while scoredchanges encourages inversions ([scoredchanges.com](https://www.scoredchanges.com/how-to/backings.pdf)). Where a separate bass role supplies the root, the inversion view is the right one. The ban on parallel octaves is sourced. The ban on parallel fifths comes from common-practice voice leading and from the harmonisation literature, where Schottstaedt gives prohibitions infinite cost, "very bad" infractions 200 and "bad" ones 100 ([Pachet & Roy](https://www.francoispachet.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/pachet-01-Musical_Harmonization_with_Constraints.pdf)). Parts declared as doublings are exempt.

### Balance: the only numeric table is Rimsky-Korsakov's

In forte, **1 trumpet = 1 trombone = 1 tuba = 2 horns**, and **1 horn = 2 flutes = 2 oboes = 2 clarinets = 2 bassoons**. In piano all winds are roughly equal. A string section such as Violins I equals one wind in piano and two in forte. Horns are marked one dynamic level above the other brass to balance ([Rimsky-Korsakov](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm)). "The melody should obviously predominate over the backings" ([Taming the Saxophone](https://tamingthesaxophone.com/theory/arranging/jazz-backings)). Rubin balances by instrument choice before dynamics ([Rubin](https://www.d.umn.edu/~jrubin1/JHR%20Orchestration%20I1.htm)). Band pedagogy has an open dispute. McBeth's bass-heavy "pyramid" became the school standard, and critics say it buries melodies ([marchingartseducation.com](https://marchingartseducation.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Ch.2_QuestionofBalance.pdf)). For a melody-teaching app, melody-first balance is the obvious choice. No source gives decibel values or weights for plucked folk instruments. The Turkish source supplies relative loudness only: kemane is "the most clearly audible" and needs restraint, mey is quieter than kaval and kemane, and other percussion must not overpower the bendir or koltuk davulu ([Kınık 2011](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/219568)).

## Texture carries the form: energy rises by addition, falls before the climax

Arrangers make form audible mainly through texture. Sound On Sound's options are concrete. Intros can fade one instrumental part in from silence or open straight on a riff. Repeated choruses vary through new chords or a detour. String parts can start low and climb toward the ending. Endings must be decided in advance: a dead stop, an unaccompanied phrase, instruments leaving one at a time, or a strip-back and rebuild to a big finish ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/arranging-pop)). Sometimes removing an element adds more impact than adding one ([ProSoundWeb](https://www.prosoundweb.com/in-the-studio-considering-song-arrangement-elements/)). Ravel's *Boléro* is the canonical additive model. One melody repeats about 18 times, starting on solo winds and moving to groups, with a constant snare ostinato. Instruments that have had their turn on the melody often join the accompaniment, and Ravel called the whole design an "orchestral crescendo" ([Wikiwand](https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Bolero_(Ravel)); [Pacific Symphony](https://pacificsymphony.blog/2017/12/19/listen-to-this-bolero/)). The march gives the band version. The trio is the contrasting, **lighter** section, usually in the subdominant, and is often followed by a tutti restatement ([Wikipedia: Trio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trio_(music)); [UHS Band](https://uhsband.com/musichistory/romantic/marchform.php)). Berklee adds one micro-level device: a pad can begin without vibrato and gain it toward the climax ([Berklee](https://www.berklee.edu/node/2436901)).

The computational evidence supports the same plan. AccoMontage's transition term rewards **reusing the same accompaniment for same-label phrases** and weights transition smoothness at 0.7 against local fit at 0.3 ([AccoMontage](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.11213)). AccoMontage-3's model learned to add a piano arpeggio in the second chorus to build momentum ([Zhao et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.16334)). Arranger keyboards encode the energy dimension as Main A–D variations, with fills "specially programmed to match" the current section and leading into the next ([Yamaha Tyros manual](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/196986/Yamaha-Tyros.html?page=34)). Together these give a five-level energy curve. E1 is the intro or outro, with one or two roles. E2 is the first verse: melody, bass and a light harmony. E3 adds rhythmic subdivision or a comp. E4 is the full four roles with a countermelody in the gaps. E5 is tutti, with doublings spread across the register. In strophic songs, the usual case for a melody file, each repeat climbs a level, the melody can rotate to a new instrument in the Boléro manner, and the previous melody instrument moves to counter or pad instead of dropping out. The penultimate section or its last bar drops one or two levels before the final E5. **No source gives bar counts** for intros or strip-backs. Two to four bars of intro and one to two bars of strip-back are conventions, not citations.

## Each family has a small, codable idiom set

Keyboard accompaniment reduces to a handful of figures documented with examples. The four block-chord rhythms are "1 (2) &", the "Barbara Ann" rhythm, repeated eighths and repeated quarters ([LibreTexts 14.4](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Theory/Music_Theory_for_the_21st-Century_Classroom_(Hutchinson)/14%3A_Accompanimental_Textures/14.04%3A_Block_Chord_Accompaniments)). Arpeggios run over an octave bass, rising or rising-and-falling, and the Alberti bass runs **low–high–middle–high** ([LibreTexts 14.3](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Theory/Music_Theory_for_the_21st-Century_Classroom_(Hutchinson)/14%3A_Accompanimental_Textures/14.03%3A_Arpeggiated_Accompaniments)). Stride puts a bass note on 1 and 3 and chords on 2 and 4 ([MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/stride-piano-guide)). Logic's Keyboard Player exposes nearly the same list to users: **Freely, Broken, Block, Arpeggiated, Pad**, and either hand can be switched off ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/logic-pro-session-players)). Guitar has down-strokes on the beats and lighter up-strokes on the "&"s. Folk backup is bass on 1 and 3 with strums on 2 and 4 (in waltz, bass then two strums), alternating bass strings, and treble strums on the top three strings. In 6/8 the strum is down-up accented on 1 and 4 ([Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Guitar/Simple_three-quarter_beat); [Celtic Guitar Music](https://celticguitarmusic.com/tb_backup_styles.htm)). Travis picking alternates the thumb on two bass strings every quarter note while the fingers fill between ([12fret](https://archive.12fret.com/news-and-reviews/moving-from-beginner-fingerpicking-techniques-to-travis-picking/)). The popular "down down-up up-down-up" pattern, palm-muted eighths and power-chord conventions are standard teaching but went uncited.

The bass puts the root on the downbeat and a chord tone on the secondary strong beat. In a walking line, beat 4 is an approach note a half step or scale step from the next root ([Fundamental Changes](https://www.fundamental-changes.com/walking-basslines-on-guitar/); [The Jazz Piano Site](https://www.thejazzpianosite.com/?p=483)). Band tubas carry real bass lines, doubled an octave up by baritones or trombones, and repeated quarters beat whole notes for drive ([ArrangeMe](https://blog.arrangeme.com/blog/arranging-for-marching-and-pep-bands)). Bowed pads are long "goose eggs" with staggered bow changes, voiced close in the middle register. High violin pads pull focus from the melody ([Berklee](https://www.berklee.edu/node/2436901)). A string countermelody must be "a melody in its own right", not restated chords ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/arranging-pop)). A countermelody uses contrary motion and steps, its own register and contrasting rhythm, and avoids parallel perfect intervals with the tune ([Orphiq](https://orphiq.com/resources/counterpoint)). In the march idiom, horns play afterbeats: one march is called unusual precisely because its horns have no afterbeat rhythms ([The Band Post](https://thebandpost.com/?p=19280)). The melody goes to the most easily heard sections, and glockenspiel sounds two octaves above written ([ArrangeMe](https://blog.arrangeme.com/blog/arranging-for-marching-and-pep-bands); [Wikipedia: Glockenspiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glockenspiel)). Concert-band percussion is used mainly for ostinato and reinforcement, rarely for melody ([UConn dissertation](https://digitalcommons.lib.uconn.edu/dissertations/AAI8125451)). The tuba root–fifth pattern, brass hits on phrase ends and marimba arpeggios are uncontroversial practice, but their citations are indirect at best.

## Turkish ensembles invert the model: one melody, many renderings, a continuous usul

The key fact for the engine is that Turkish makam and folk ensembles are **heterophonic**. Everyone plays versions of the same line with their own embellishment, and the texture comes from those differences plus percussion, not from chords ([Wikipedia: Ottoman music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_music); [tousauxbalkans.net](https://www.tousauxbalkans.net/Cat%C3%A9gorie:Classique_ottoman)). In code, heterophony means passing one source melody through a transform for each instrument: an octave offset, re-striking or tremolo on long notes for plucked instruments, sustain and slides for winds and bowed strings, and less ornament for supporting instruments than for the lead. The transforms are an inference from the descriptions; no source gives the algorithm. Makam scales do not always repeat identically in the upper octave ([rsexp blog](https://rsexp.blogspot.com/2019/07/traditional-music-of-turkey.html)). Airfret's 12-TET instruments lose that nuance anyway, so exact octave transposition is the honest approximation.

Kınık's academic study of THM ensembles is the anchor source. **Bağlamas voice the "ana parti" (main part) and are mostly in the leading role**. Kara düzen tuning gives natural I–IV–V open-string sonorities, and the Karadeniz kemençe "is polyphonic by nature", bowing drones and parallel fourths ([Kınık 2011, p. 218](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/219568)). The bağlama family forms a choir. Cura, tambura and divan together cover **3.5 octaves** on their first strings, and adding bas bağlama brings the total to about **5 octaves**. The study maps them explicitly onto Western strings: **violin = cura or tambura, viola = tambura, cello = divan, double bass = bas bağlama or divan**. The ensemble-standard "Fa" instruments transpose as follows: **Fa tambura sounds a fifth below written, Fa divan a fifth plus an octave below, and Fa cura a fourth above**. Cura "most effectively meets the need for high notes" ([Kınık 2011, pp. 224–229](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/219568)). Kaval "meets the need for long tones in the ensemble" and blends with kemane. Kaval should drop out of high passages where its intonation is risky, mey suits slurred lines but not fast ones, and bas kopuz supplies sustained bowed bass. **Bendir and koltuk davulu play from the beginning to the end of the piece**, and other percussion joins "according to the course of the piece" without overpowering them. Kınık concludes that the bass voice is "always needed", which is why bas bağlama became "inevitable" ([Kınık 2011, pp. 226–232](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/219568)). Modern arranged THM uses either Western harmony or Kemal İlerici's **quartal "dörtlü armoni"**. Its rules are explicit: contrary motion as much as possible, no successive dissonances, **no more than two perfect fourths or fifths in a row**, half cadences on a fifth, **final cadence on a fourth** (or a fifth, unison or octave), and resolutions 2nd→4th, 7th→5th, 9th→octave ([Kınık 2011, pp. 219–220](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/219568)).

Fasıl and classical ensembles (tanbur, ney, kemençe, ud, kanun, keman) play the composed melody heterophonically. **Darbuka is the most important percussion in fasıl**, while kudüm belongs to the classical and Mevlevi setting ([CompMusic](https://compmusic.upf.edu/index.php/node/55)). Instrumental taksims open a fasıl and punctuate it ([Ottoman classical music, Wikipedia mirror](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Ottoman_Classical_Music.html)). Who formally leads a fasıl went unsourced. The mehter is built on the zurna, "the most fundamental music instrument of the mehter band", including the low kaba zurna. Its percussion of davul, nakkare, kös and zil was as large as the melodic section, multiplied by the "kat" count: a nine-kat band had nine each of davul, zurna, nakkare, zil and boru, plus one or two kös ([Wikipedia: Ottoman military band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_military_band)). Ceng-i harbî began as the clash of cymbals and became a mehter usul name ([TDV: Zil](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/zil)), but its strokes were not found. Davul-zurna accompanies "almost all folk dances in Anatolia". The **tokmak** (right hand, thick skin) plays the main accents, and the **çubuk** (left hand, thin skin) "maintains the rhythm on the opposite side" ([TDV: Davul](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/davul)). Düm and tek map onto those two voices, as "right hand düm, left hand tek" on any instrument ([MEB kanun module](https://tymm.meb.gov.tr/muzik-okullari-bireysel-calgi-egitimi-dersi-kanun-modulu/unite/1277)).

Usul patterns are only partly verified. Karaol and Tunçer give present-day strokes for **düyek (8/8) `D T – T D – T –`, semai `D T T`, devr-i hindi (7/8) `D T T D – T –`, and evfer (9/8) `D – T k D – T T –`** ([Karaol & Tunçer 2015](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/150662)). The aksak family is the same strokes at three tempos (9/16 yürük aksak, 9/8 aksak, 9/4 ağır aksak, the zeybek usul), grouped 2+2+2+3 ([TDV: Yürük aksak](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/yuruk-aksak); [Wikipedia: Aksak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksak)). Full strokes for sofyan, nim sofyan, türk aksağı, yürük semai, aksak, aksak semai, curcuna and ceng-i harbî are **unverified**. One web source (chromatone.center) was rejected because it gave aksak semai as 2+2+2+2+2.

The national marches are a separate case. İstiklal Marşı is Üngör's melody, which **Edgar Manas** was commissioned in 1932 to harmonise and orchestrate, and the official recording is in **G minor** ([Wikipedia: İstiklal Marşı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0stiklal_Mar%C5%9F%C4%B1)). İzmir Marşı's composer is disputed ([Habertürk, Bardakçı](https://www.haberturk.com/yazarlar/murat-bardakci/1380237-izmir-marsi-muammasi)). Both are Western-tonal marches. They should get functional harmony and brass-band scoring, with Turkish instruments added only as mehter-style unison doublings of the melody and davul and zil on the strong beats. İlerici's quartal rules should not be applied to them.

## Rule systems succeed when the plan is fixed first and patterns are translated

The machine-learning orchestrators are, underneath, note-assignment systems wrapped in rules. Nabeoka's wind-band system post-processes its network with four hard rules: notes must come from the piano input, pitch ranges come "from standard books on orchestration", each part is monophonic, and durations are copied. Even so, its best F-score was only about **36–37%** on exact match ([Nabeoka et al.](https://zenodo.org/records/10113109/files/cmmr2023_3c-P5.pdf)). The behaviour it learned is worth copying: **woodwinds took the right-hand notes, brass the left, and chords were voiced "in the natural order of the parts"** with no crossing. On the projective-orchestration benchmark, copying the previous frame was the strongest baseline, and the neural models predicted almost every note as off ([Crestel et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1810.08611)). Note-match against a single reference is therefore a poor quality measure. The constraint-harmonisation literature solved four-part voicing by about 2001, and its main lesson is structural. Fix a harmonisation plan first, which leaves "only the actual octaves" to solve, or add chord-level variables; either makes the search "practically real time". The same survey warns that rules are "merely syntactic" and do not guarantee interest, and that keyboard one-touch harmonisation is "note-to-chord, reactive … without taking into account the context" ([Pachet & Roy](https://www.francoispachet.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/pachet-01-Musical_Harmonization_with_Constraints.pdf)). Rule-based harmonisation beat genetic algorithms on solution quality ([Pachet & Roy](https://www.francoispachet.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/pachet-01-Musical_Harmonization_with_Constraints.pdf)).

Yamaha's style engine is the deterministic real-time model that maps most directly onto TypeScript. Each part is a pattern recorded on a source chord (Cmaj7). An **NTR** rule decides how the root moves: ROOT TRANS moves the pattern in parallel up to a High Key, ROOT FIXED stays "as close as possible to the previous note range", and GUITAR approximates natural fingering. An **NTT** table decides how the chord quality maps (Bypass for drums, Melody, Chord, Bass, Arpeggio, and minor variants that lower only the third). An **RTR** rule decides what a held note does at a chord change: stop, pitch shift, retrigger, or move to the root. A Note Limit folds notes into the part's range ([Yamaha 301 manual](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/196615/Yamaha-301.html?page=119); [PSR-8000 manual](https://www.manualowl.com/m/Yamaha/PSR-8000/Manual/152595?page=72); [Tyros3 manual](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/477787/Yamaha-Tyros3.html?page=46)). What it lacks is context, and AccoMontage supplies that: a Viterbi path over phrases with a fitness term of 0.5 × rhythm cosine + 0.5 × chord-TIV cosine, a transition term, and the form rule that repeats reuse textures ([AccoMontage](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.11213)). Airfret's arranger should therefore be pattern selection for each phrase with a smoothness and form term, followed by Yamaha-style deterministic note translation and a small cost-minimising voicing search for each chord.

Evaluation should rely on constraint-violation rates rather than reference matching. The research uses listening tests on coherence, structure, naturalness, creativity, musicality and instrumentation ([Zhao et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.16334)), plus objective proxies. These include **chord accuracy** (0.567 for AccoMontage-3 against 0.423–0.470 for baselines), **degree of arrangement** (the spread of tracks' pitch-class divergence from the reduction), **structure awareness** (similarity of same-label phrases relative to the average) and **faithfulness** ([Zhao et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.16334)). The MuseGAN statistics cover empty bars, used pitch classes, qualified-note rate (fragmentation), polyphonicity and inter-track tonal distance ([MuseGAN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.06298); [muspy](https://muspy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/doc/metrics.html)). Nabeoka also checks co-occurrence and exclusion between parts ([Nabeoka et al.](https://zenodo.org/records/10113109/files/cmmr2023_3c-P5.pdf)). Voice-crossing rate and semitone-clash rate are not established field metrics, but they turn the orchestration rules above directly into tests. They fit Airfret's existing fixture-based `test:songs` discipline.

## Users want one style knob, a role per instrument, and depth hidden

Every commercial precedent has four layers of control. First, a whole-band **style or preset** sets everything: the Band-in-a-Box style, Yamaha's One Touch Setting, the iReal Pro style or a Logic preset. Second, **on/off and level for each instrument** is always visible. Third, an instrument's **character** comes from one or two continuous controls or a short ordered list. Fourth, details sit behind a disclosure ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/logic-pro-session-players); [Genos manual](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1309559/Yamaha-Genos.html?page=47); [iReal Pro](https://www.irealpro.com/learn/customize-your-backing-tracks)). Apple's model draws the most praise for its consistency. GarageBand's XY puck sets **simple ↔ complex** horizontally and **soft ↔ loud** vertically ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/garageband-iphone/chs9692b2133/ios)), and the same controls (Complexity, Intensity, Fills, Swing, with Details behind a disclosure) appear on every Session Player. Arrangement-track markers vary the parts automatically for each section, and "Follow" locks one part's rhythm to another's ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/logic-pro-session-players)). Band-in-a-Box contributes the role filter (**Rhythm / Background / Soloist**), the complexity steps (**"Simpler"** and **"Held"**, the simplest comping part), a/b substyles and "Find a Sub" ranked alternatives. Its exposed depth is also the field's most persistent complaint: "a forest of hidden commands, pop-up menus, and dialog boxes" ([PG Music](https://www.pgmusic.com/manuals/bbw2026full/chapter7.htm); [Synth and Software](https://synthandsoftware.com/2021/11/band-in-a-box-2021-the-synth-and-software-review/)). Hookpad regenerates the arrangement in an instrument's idiom whenever the instrument is swapped ([Hookpad](https://www.hooktheory.com/hookpad)), so role-to-pattern defaults should depend on the instrument. iReal Pro's core practice use is muting the part you want to play yourself ([iReal Pro](https://www.irealpro.com/learn/customize-your-backing-tracks)), and that is exactly Airfret's situation. No product has a "heterophony" preset. The nearest mechanisms are Follow and Band-in-a-Box's stacking of several tracks. No usage data was found showing which controls users actually change.

## Specification: a prioritised arranging system

Status key: **S** = sourced (cited above), **I** = engineering inference built from sourced findings, **U** = unverified practitioner convention to check before shipping. Priority: **P0** = needed for "doesn't sound like a jumble", **P1** = needed for "sounds arranged", **P2** = polish.

### Roles and their contracts

| Role | Contract | Default instrument affinity | Pri | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `melody` | Plays every melody note of the phrase, folded by octaves into range; one per phrase | Strongest or most characterful sustaining instrument; bağlama in THM; zurna or trumpet in mehter | P0 | S |
| `double` | Melody at unison (power) or octave; **above** for brightness, below for body; counts as the same element | Cura, flute, glock, violin (+8va); divan, cello (−8va) | P0 | S |
| `heterophony` | Melody through an idiom transform: octave from the register table, re-strike or tremolo (plucked) or sustain and slide (wind, bowed), ornament density below the lead's | Kanun, ud, saz family, ney, kemençe, keman | P0 for Turkish presets | I (concept S) |
| `counter` | Single line in its own register and family; moves where the melody holds or rests, holds where it moves; contrary or stepwise motion; at most one | Clarinet, horn, cello, kaval; enabled at E3+ | P1 | S |
| `harmony` (comp) | Chord tones in a pattern on the afterbeats or offbeats, or broken; voice-led | Guitar, piano, saz strums, horns (march) | P0 | S |
| `pad` (held harmony) | Sustained voicing, one attack per chord (re-pulsed at E4+), close voicing in the middle register below the melody | Bowed strings, organ, winds, brass at E4–5 only | P0 | S |
| `drone` | Karar (tonic) with optional fifth or fourth, held | Kemençe, kaval, second zurna, bowed bass | P2 | I / U (zurna dem) |
| `bass` | Lowest voice; root on strong beats; doubled only an octave below; within about an octave of the next voice up | Bass guitar, tuba, cello, bas bağlama, bas kopuz | P0 | S |
| `punctuation` | Stabs or fills only in melody rests and holds, at most 1–2 per bar, at E4–5 | Brass, piano, kanun runs | P2 | S (placement) / U (density) |
| `rhythm` | Usul or groove for the whole piece; düm → low voice, tek → high voice | Bendir or koltuk davulu (THM), darbuka (fasıl), davul (folk, mehter), kit (Western) | P0 | S |
| `tacet` | Silent this section (an explicit role, so texture plans can thin) | Any | P0 | I |

| Pattern | Definition | Use | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| `held` | One attack per chord | E1–E2, ballads, pads | S |
| `afterbeat` | Chords on the "&"s, or on 2 and 4 against a bass on 1 and 3; waltz: 2 and 3 | Marches, folk, polka | S |

### Per-section texture plan (energy E1–E5)

| Energy | Section examples | Active distinct roles (cap) | Typical content | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Intro, outro | 1–2 | Pad or arpeggio alone, or melody alone; or the last 2–4 bars of the tune over chords | S (shape), U (bar count) |
| E2 | Verse 1, march trio | 3 | Melody + bass + `held` or sparse comp | S |
| E3 | Verse 2, pre-chorus | 3–4 | Add a subdivision (quarters to eighths) or `counter` | I |
| E4 | Chorus, march strain repeat | 4 | Melody (+`double`) + bass + comp + pad; counter only in gaps | S |
| E5 | Final chorus, grandioso | 4 roles, all instruments | Tutti doubling into the 4 roles; bass −8va, glock or high strings +8va | S |
| Strip-back | Bar(s) before the final E5 | 1–2 levels below the previous section | Drop bass and drums, or pad + melody only | S (device), U (length) |
| Ending | Last bar | — | March: tutti "button" on beat 1; ballad: held fermata or subtractive exit in reverse entry order | S |

Repeat rules: same-label sections reuse the same patterns (form term, **S**). Verse *n* gets energy `min(5, base + n − 1)` (**I**). On a repeat, the melody may rotate to the next instrument in an affinity order, and the previous melody instrument becomes `counter` or `pad` (Boléro, **S**). For Turkish presets, the bendir or davul usul runs throughout, and darbuka or def enter only at E3+ (**S**).

| Item | Value | Status |

## Conclusion

The research changes what the arranging problem looks like. Clarity in an ensemble mostly comes not from voice-leading finesse but from **budgeting**: a cap on distinct roles, an onset grid that keeps attacks out of each other's way, a register lane per role, and a weight budget that keeps every role under the tune. Each of these can be checked mechanically, so Airfret can enforce the "not a jumble" property as a test gate, not as taste. The rule systems' known weakness is blandness, not wrong notes, and texture across the form answers it: energy levels, Boléro rotation, the strip-back and planned endings. Variety therefore has to be designed in as explicitly as the constraints. A pattern library alone will sound like an arranger keyboard on Main A.

The second insight is that Turkish repertoire is a different texture, not a different preset. A heterophony mode that writes one melody in several idiomatic renderings over a continuous usul is both more authentic and easier to keep clear than forcing kanun, saz and ney into Western pad-and-comp roles. The Kınık register map (cura, tambura, divan, bas bağlama as violin, viola, cello, bass) lets the same engine cross between the two modes. The weakest parts of the specification are sixth-to-seventh LIL values, plucked-instrument balance, most usul stroke grids, bar counts for intros and strip-backs, and decibel offsets. These should ship behind constants that can be adjusted by ear and replaced as primary sources (Özkan or Ungay on usul, a full LIL chart, listening tests) are checked.
